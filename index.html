Full abstract

Matching and reconstructing a 3D scene across pinhole, fisheye, catadioptric and panoramic cameras is essential for robots, autonomous vehicles and multi-camera rigs. However, existing dense matchers and 3D foundation models assume pinhole projection, so wide-angle distortion breaks cross-camera correspondences and makes predicted rays deviate from calibration. Although retraining backbones on ray maps or adding camera adapters reduces this mismatch, it requires multi-camera training data and leaves output rays unconstrained on unseen lenses and mixed fields of view. To address these challenges, we propose UniCam3R on a frozen pinhole backbone, whose Unified Ray Camera Model renders virtual perspective views matched to the narrower camera for training-free inference and gates LoRA updates using the projection Jacobian so that pinhole outputs remain unchanged. Across unknown overlap, our Cross-View Overlap Verification removes the selection bias of chosen virtual views via two-fold spatial cross-validation with epipolar and reprojection checks. Along calibrated rays, our Along-Ray Depth Alignment models shared log-depth offsets per spherical cell to bound the weight of dense match clusters and eliminates these depth offsets in closed form across pairs, pose graphs and depth fusion. Experiments on real and synthetic mixed-camera benchmarks demonstrate that UniCam3R achieves state-of-the-art cross-camera matching, relative pose and 3D reconstruction, while transferring across frozen backbones without training and supporting streaming monocular SLAM. Code and pretrained models will be publicly released.

02Ground Everything on Calibrated Rays

Although lens projections warp 2D grids differently, calibration maps every pixel p to a known unit bearing vector r(p) and writes each 3D point as X(p) = ρ(p) r(p) with a 1D depth along that ray. Once correspondences connect calibrated rays and 3D points lie on r(p), mixed-camera reconstruction reduces to spherical matching and estimating poses and ray depths on a frozen pinhole backbone.

03Method

Swipe horizontally to inspect the full architecture

Figure 2. Overview of UniCam3R. Given calibrated input images on the left, our Unified Ray Camera Model maps each central camera onto the unit sphere to render virtual perspective views matched to the narrower camera, while a gating mask computed from the projection Jacobian scales a ray MLP and LoRA updates on the frozen backbone so that pinhole outputs remain unchanged. In the center, Cross-View Overlap Verification generates candidate virtual views and evaluates them by two-fold cross-validation across disjoint spatial folds. On the right, Along-Ray Depth Alignment models shared depth offsets in spherical cells and eliminates them by block elimination across pairwise depth alignment, global pose graph optimization and depth fusion into a 3D point cloud.

Bounded cell weight. Marginalizing the offset of a cell with n residuals keeps full precision on depth differences inside the cell while bounding the precision of the cell mean below the prior λβ. Independent pixel weights let dense clusters bias forward translation, and one scale per image cannot follow drift across a wide view; on short-baseline KITTI-360 pairs, regional offsets reach 0.867 AUC@10 against 0.751 for a single scale.

06Ablation

  1. Cross-View Overlap VerificationRemoving verification admits false matches on large rotations and repetitive textures, lowering mean AUC@10 from 0.868 to 0.836, ScanNet++ from 0.879 to 0.805, and pairs rotated above 90° from 0.874 to 0.737.
  2. Along-Ray Depth AlignmentBenefiting from 5° cells that explain 50–84% of log-depth error variance across lenses, cell depth offsets keep short-baseline KITTI-360 at 0.878 against 0.808 with one scale per view, and removing virtual views and depth alignment drops mean AUC@10 to 0.698.
  3. Unified Ray Camera ModelRemoving gated LoRA lowers whole-image mean AUC@10 to 0.398 and KITTI-360 to 0.206, while its gating mask keeps pinhole pairs at 0.893 against 0.857 without gating, and removing ray lifting leaves the raw backbone at 0.008.

08Supplementary Material

The supplementary material provides the following details beyond the main paper.

S1Properties of the Ray-Space Model

Exactness of the gating mask on pinhole cameras and comparison with pixel-radius gating, the information of one cell block, the exact reduction to a 7×7 system with pairwise gauge observability, the marginal information of a pair, and the posterior of the dense range.

S2Implementation Details

The UniRay network and calibrated ray loss, candidate perspective views and solver, Along-Ray Depth Alignment and fusion, Cross-View Overlap Verification, the multi-view module, gate training, and thermal and event cameras.

S3Data and Protocol

Multi-view subsets of ScanNet++ (12 DSLR fisheye frames, up to 6 panoramas and 8 phone frames per scene) and 360Loc, ground truth, evaluation protocol and baseline settings.

S4The Camera Inferred by Models Trained on Pinhole Images

The single pinhole camera fitted to the predicted directions of MASt3R, DUSt3R, π3 and MapAnything on Replica validation pairs.

S5Relative Pose of 3D Foundation Models

Pose AUC@10 of 3D foundation models on the pairs of Table 1, where models trained on pinhole images stay below 0.1 and Any3R reaches at most 0.169 to 0.309.

S6Cross-Device Relocalization

Localizing images from other cameras in a panoramic 360Loc map, with atrium for development and concourse, hall and piatrium for testing.

S7Agreement Between Lenses

Relative 3D discrepancy between seven lenses, from a 60° pinhole to a full panorama, rendered at the same Replica poses.

S8MASt3R with a Monocular Metric Prior

MASt3R pairs scaled by UniK3D metric depths and solved by our calibrated solver, evaluated for two-view 3D consistency.

S9Further Ablations

Dense depth fusion, directions as an output constraint, view layout, viewpoint change, the projection-Jacobian gating mask and calibrated ray loss, module design breakdowns, and depth error structure.

S10Focal-Length Errors

Two-view pose under randomly signed focal-length errors from 0.2% to 2% on Replica New, against RoMa and Backbone + Ray Lifting.

S11Relative Pose by Camera Combination

Replica New pairs split by the cameras of their two views across eight camera combinations.

S12Multi-View Accuracy by Camera Combination

Two-view poses against poses after the pose graph for every camera combination of the ScanNet++ test scenes, with phone pairs rising from 0.431 to 0.953 AUC@30.

S13Mixed Lenses on Identical Views

The same view triplets from four unused Replica scenes rendered with seven lens configurations, so that only the lenses change between rows.

S14Number of Images

All methods on 6, 12 and 18 images per ScanNet++ test scene, drawn in proportion to the three cameras.

S15Larger Scenes

Three ScanNet++ test scenes enlarged to 59–62 images and 5,372 pairs, where UniCam3R reaches 0.029 m ATE and 0.974 AUC@30.

S16Multi-View Focal-Length Errors

Focal-length errors on every DSLR and phone camera of five ScanNet++ test scenes, with equirectangular panoramas kept exact.

S17Dense Reconstruction on 360Loc

Dense point clouds against the laser ranges of the mapping panoramas at 5 cm and 25 cm thresholds.

S18Fisheye Scenes

Single-lens reconstruction on the 12 DSLR fisheye views of every ScanNet++ test scene against Wid3R and multi-view foundation models.

S19Comparison with Any3R

The Any3R protocol on 50 ScanNet++ fisheye triplets and twelve 200° FIORD triplets.

S20Sixteen Cameras in Unseen Replica Apartments

Six held-out FRL apartments in which each of sixteen viewpoints uses a different lens.

S21Comparison under the Camera3R Protocol

16-view mixed-camera sets on 360Loc and KITTI-360, rebuilt by the stated rule of Camera3R.

S22Training-Free Cross-Backbone Generalization

UniRay perspective rendering and ray lifting on frozen VGGT and π3 without retraining, on ScanNet++ and 360Loc development scenes.

S23Cross-Sensor Pairs

Color-thermal pairs of MS2 and color-event pairs of DSEC against RoMa, MINIMA-RoMa and XoFTR, with qualitative correspondences.

S24Monocular SLAM Gate and Three-Camera Switching Ablation
